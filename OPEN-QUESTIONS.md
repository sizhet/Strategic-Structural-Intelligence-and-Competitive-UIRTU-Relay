# Open Questions — Strategic Structural Intelligence and Competitive UIRTU-Relay (SSICR)

**Authors:** Sizhe Tan & GPT-Obot  
**Project:** Strategic Structural Intelligence and Competitive UIRTU-Relay (SSICR)  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Open research agenda; questions and hypotheses, not established findings  
**Date:** 2026-10-26

---

## Abstract

Strategic Structural Intelligence and Competitive UIRTU-Relay (SSICR) investigates how intelligent subjects with distinct goals, authority boundaries, capabilities, and worldviews can compete, cooperate, simulate alternatives, and learn through structured Relay relationships. This document records unresolved scientific, architectural, empirical, and governance questions. It distinguishes questions that can be addressed with controlled simulation from those requiring observational evidence, participatory evaluation, or normative authorization. A central problem is how to convert AI-scale strategic exploration into reliable, intelligible, contestable knowledge while retaining human strategic sovereignty.

**Keywords:** strategic structural intelligence; competitive Relay; multi-agent simulation; comparative advantage; strategic advantage map; worldview forest; open-ended RSI; counter-evidence; OTHERS; human strategic sovereignty.

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This is a living research agenda, not a list of presumed solutions. Each question is accompanied by a potential research approach and a falsification or failure criterion where possible. SSICR deliberately separates:

- **Descriptive questions:** What occurs in a specified model or observed system?
- **Predictive questions:** What generalizes to unseen opponents, contexts, and times?
- **Design questions:** Which contracts and algorithms reliably improve an operational objective?
- **Normative questions:** Who is entitled to set objectives, accept risks, and authorize actions?

No simulation can settle a normative question merely by producing a higher score.

## 2. Foundational Questions: What Is a Strategic Subject?

### OQ-001 — What constitutes a strategic subject?

Is an individual agent, team, organization, institution, or coalition a strategic subject when it has coherent objectives, an action boundary, and an identifiable authority? How should nested and overlapping subjects be represented?

**Research approach:** Compare subject definitions against cases involving delegated agents, jointly owned resources, and temporary coalitions.  
**Failure signal:** A proposed subject model cannot attribute decisions, permissions, or consequences without contradictions.

### OQ-002 — How are subject boundaries discovered and revised?

Can boundaries be inferred from observed decision rights and resource flows, or must they always be explicitly declared? How are contested boundaries handled?

**Research approach:** Test explicit contracts against inferred boundary models and document disagreement.  
**Failure signal:** Inference silently enlarges an agent's authorized scope.

### OQ-003 — Can multiple scoring sovereignties coexist within one institution?

How should a strategic organization represent incompatible preferences among members, shareholders, regulators, and affected communities?

**Research approach:** Model distinct stakeholder scoring perspectives without forcing premature aggregation.  
**Failure signal:** The system reports one “institutional optimum” while concealing unresolved value conflicts.

### OQ-004 — What is the smallest useful strategic UIRTU-Relay unit?

At what granularity do modular strategic units improve reuse, testing, and adaptation rather than creating prohibitive coordination overhead?

**Research approach:** Ablate modularity levels while controlling for compute and task complexity.

## 3. Competitive Relay and Strategic Interaction

### OQ-005 — When does Relay change from cooperative to competitive?

Can a single Relay contract express cooperation, bargaining, rivalry, deception risk, and mixed-motive exchange without ambiguous semantics?

**Research approach:** Specify interaction modes, permitted information flows, and enforcement tests.  
**Failure signal:** A cooperative permission is implicitly inherited in an adversarial context.

### OQ-006 — What determines the advantage of open, closed, and hybrid Relay?

How do information sensitivity, modularity, trust, interoperability, innovation rate, and dependency risk affect strategic performance?

**Research approach:** Factorial experiments across contexts and opponent policies.  
**Failure signal:** A strategy's apparent advantage disappears under plausible held-out contexts.

### OQ-007 — How should reciprocity and trust be modeled?

Are trust scores meaningful across changing partners, incentives, and institutions? What prevents manipulation of reputation or evidence provenance?

**Research approach:** Compare reputation-based, contract-based, and verification-based Relay under adversarial perturbations.

### OQ-008 — How do coalitions and institutions emerge from local Relay choices?

Under what conditions do repeated exchanges produce stable standards, alliances, shared evaluation infrastructure, or exclusionary structures?

**Research approach:** Longitudinal agent-based experiments with institutional rules that can themselves change.

### OQ-009 — What makes a Relay arrangement robust to strategic exploitation?

Can a Relay be both interoperable and resistant to data poisoning, strategic withholding, or unauthorized control transfer?

**Research approach:** Red-team information and authorization boundaries, measuring both utility and violation rates.

## 4. Massive Multi-Strategy Simulation

### OQ-010 — How much simulation is enough?

How can a system determine when further strategic exploration has low marginal information value, rather than merely low score improvement?

**Research approach:** Compare stopping rules based on ranking stability, uncertainty reduction, and expected decision value.

### OQ-011 — How should compute be allocated across strategies and scenarios?

Can adaptive allocation outperform uniform allocation when some strategies are promising but poorly understood?

**Research approach:** Equal-budget comparisons of uniform search, bandit allocation, active learning, and uncertainty-targeted search.  
**Failure signal:** Adaptive allocation systematically neglects rare high-impact failure modes.

### OQ-012 — How can opponent adaptation be modeled without circular overfitting?

Self-play and cross-play can produce policies specialized to the simulated opponent population. How can unseen opponent classes be evaluated?

**Research approach:** Use held-out policies, independently designed opponents, behavioral shifts, and external benchmarks.

### OQ-013 — Which simulation fidelity is necessary for which decision?

When can a coarse structural simulator rank strategic alternatives reliably, and when are higher-fidelity mechanisms indispensable?

**Research approach:** Multi-fidelity comparisons with explicit error bounds and decision reversals.

### OQ-014 — How do we distinguish simulation coverage from world coverage?

What evidence supports extrapolation from simulated contexts to real organizational or social systems?

**Research approach:** Track domain assumptions and compare predictions with external observations where ethically and practically feasible.  
**Failure signal:** Strong internal simulation scores with weak out-of-distribution prediction.

### OQ-015 — Can rare catastrophic outcomes be assessed reliably?

How can low-frequency, high-impact scenarios be explored when empirical data are scarce and model assumptions dominate?

**Research approach:** Stress tests, sensitivity analysis, scenario families, and conservative uncertainty reporting; avoid claiming calibrated probabilities without evidence.

## 5. Strategic Advantage Maps (SAM)

### OQ-016 — What exactly is a comparative advantage claim?

Should a claim be represented as a conditional ranking, effect size, Pareto relation, probability of superiority, or a set of these?

**Research approach:** Evaluate representational adequacy on cases with conflicting objectives and rank reversals.

### OQ-017 — How should advantage regions be discovered?

Which methods best identify contiguous contexts in which one strategy dominates a comparator, while preserving uncertainty near boundaries?

**Research approach:** Compare partition trees, surrogate models, causal models, and interpretable classifiers on synthetic ground-truth landscapes.

### OQ-018 — What counts as a genuine failure boundary?

Can the system distinguish a real change in comparative advantage from sparse sampling, simulator artifacts, or metric changes?

**Research approach:** Targeted sampling near suspected boundaries, independent model checks, and prospective tests.

### OQ-019 — How should conflicting evidence update a map?

When should evidence narrow a region, split a map, lower confidence, or invalidate a strategic claim?

**Research approach:** Pre-register revision rules and evaluate calibration after contradictory observations.

### OQ-020 — How portable are advantage maps across domains?

Which structural invariants transfer between AI R&D, organizational competition, supply networks, and other settings?

**Research approach:** Transfer experiments with explicit mapping assumptions and negative-transfer measurements.

### OQ-021 — Can a map represent non-comparability honestly?

How should maps handle strategic alternatives that cannot be ranked without normative tradeoffs or absent information?

**Research approach:** Use partial orders, incomparability states, and stakeholder-specific scoring; test whether users understand these distinctions.

## 6. From Advantage Maps to Strategic Worldview Forests

### OQ-022 — When does a repeated advantage pattern justify a principle?

What level of recurrence, mechanistic support, and external validation is sufficient to promote a map pattern into a reusable strategic principle?

**Research approach:** Compare promotion thresholds and measure false generalization on held-out contexts.

### OQ-023 — What makes a strategic worldview more than a collection of rules?

How should a worldview represent perception filters, causal assumptions, priorities, strategic heuristics, and revision policies?

**Research approach:** Test whether explicit worldview structure improves coherent reasoning and adaptation over flat rule stores.

### OQ-024 — How should incompatible worldviews coexist?

Can the system maintain distinct normative perspectives without forcing a false consensus or treating all empirical claims as equally credible?

**Research approach:** Separate evidence adjudication from value aggregation; evaluate multi-perspective decision support.

### OQ-025 — How can worldview lock-in be detected?

What observable indicators reveal selective evidence intake, self-confirming simulation design, or suppression of counter-evidence?

**Research approach:** Introduce adversarial counterexamples and measure discovery, retention, and revision behavior.

### OQ-026 — What should trigger worldview branching, merging, or retirement?

How can evolution avoid both uncontrolled proliferation and premature consolidation?

**Research approach:** Compare structural revision operators using predictive calibration, complexity, and preservation of dissenting evidence.

### OQ-027 — Can worldview evolution be meaningfully measured?

Should progress be measured by prediction, robustness, explanatory coverage, option diversity, human comprehension, or a multi-objective vector?

**Research approach:** Predefine metrics and study tradeoffs rather than collapsing them into a single unexamined score.

## 7. Open-Ended Strategic Recursive Self-Improvement

### OQ-028 — What distinguishes strategic RSI from ordinary optimization?

Does a system improve the strategy search procedure, simulator, evaluator, Relay topology, or principle-generation process—not only the selected strategy?

**Research approach:** Track changes to the improvement machinery and compare against frozen-pipeline baselines.

### OQ-029 — How can self-improvement be validated when evaluators evolve?

If both policy and evaluator change, what prevents apparent gains caused by metric drift or evaluator capture?

**Research approach:** Maintain frozen external benchmarks, independent evaluators, and historical replay suites.

### OQ-030 — Can strategic growth remain open-ended without losing coherence?

How can the system admit new strategic operators, interfaces, and evidence types while retaining provenance and contract compatibility?

**Research approach:** Versioned contracts, compatibility tests, rollback, and staged deployment.

### OQ-031 — When does collective RSI outperform isolated RSI?

Under which conditions does knowledge exchange among competing subjects produce net gains after coordination, leakage, and governance costs?

**Research approach:** Compare isolated, cooperative, competitive, and hybrid populations at equal resource budgets.

### OQ-032 — What is a safe boundary for autonomous strategic experimentation?

Which experiments can remain sandboxed, and which actions require human review because they alter external systems, rights, or institutions?

**Research approach:** Permission-tier conformance tests and explicit escalation triggers.

## 8. Social and Institutional Co-Evolution

### OQ-033 — What minimum social structure must be represented?

Which features—identity, incentives, networks, norms, institutions, learning, and resource constraints—are necessary for credible social structural simulation?

**Research approach:** Incremental model construction with ablation and external validation.

### OQ-034 — How do institutions change strategic advantage?

Can the same strategy move from beneficial to harmful as standards, governance rules, or power distributions evolve?

**Research approach:** Endogenous institution models and controlled institutional shocks.

### OQ-035 — How can model diversity prevent artificial social consensus?

Does an ensemble of independently specified agents and institutions reveal structural uncertainty hidden by one simulator?

**Research approach:** Compare heterogeneous model ensembles with single-model populations; inspect disagreement rather than average it away.

### OQ-036 — What are the limits of strategic prediction in reflexive societies?

When predictions influence behavior, can the system model the feedback without claiming unwarranted forecasting precision?

**Research approach:** Study prediction-induced behavior changes and report conditional scenarios rather than deterministic futures.

### OQ-037 — How should affected nonparticipants be represented?

How can the evaluation include harms, externalities, and rights of parties who do not control the strategic system?

**Research approach:** Stakeholder impact models, participatory review, and explicit non-compensable constraints.

## 9. Human Strategic Sovereignty and Governance

### OQ-038 — What is the operational meaning of strategic sovereignty?

Is sovereignty satisfied by a final approval click, or does it require meaningful comprehension, contestability, control over objectives, and effective revocation?

**Research approach:** Measure understanding, decision quality, override capability, and time-to-revoke in human-in-the-loop trials.

### OQ-039 — How can AI-scale strategy streams become human-comprehensible?

What compression preserves decisive alternatives, tradeoffs, failure boundaries, uncertainty, and dissenting evidence?

**Research approach:** Compare map-based summaries, Pareto frontiers, counterfactual explanations, and interactive review under controlled information budgets.

### OQ-040 — How can automation bias and authority laundering be detected?

Does an apparently neutral score or summary conceal model assumptions, omitted perspectives, or unauthorized normative choices?

**Research approach:** Blind randomized studies of presentation formats and decision-maker behavior.

### OQ-041 — Where is the boundary between epistemic and normative revision?

When a model learns that a policy fails, it may revise its prediction. Under what conditions would changing objectives, risk tolerance, or affected parties' rights require separate authorization?

**Research approach:** Formalize revision types and test authority checks on borderline cases.

### OQ-042 — Can strategic authorization remain revocable in distributed Relay systems?

How do revocation signals propagate through asynchronous agents, cached plans, downstream delegates, and already initiated actions?

**Research approach:** Fault-injection and propagation-delay tests; distinguish preventable future actions from irreversible past effects.

### OQ-043 — How can governance scale without becoming ceremonial?

Which decisions require human review, and which can be delegated under bounded policies without overwhelming human oversight?

**Research approach:** Compare risk-tiered delegation with universal approval and unrestricted autonomy.

## 10. Counter-Evidence, OTHERS, and Epistemic Reliability

### OQ-044 — How can unknown unknowns become actionable evidence?

Can anomalous outcomes, unexplained residuals, and adversarial examples be routed into a structured OTHERS evidence space rather than discarded?

**Research approach:** Measure discovery rates and subsequent predictive gains on planted and naturally occurring anomalies.

### OQ-045 — How should counter-evidence compete with confirming evidence?

How can an evaluator resist selective sampling, strategic manipulation, and Goodhart effects?

**Research approach:** Adversarial evidence injection, independent review, and pre-registered evidence weighting.

### OQ-046 — Can evidence provenance survive multi-hop Relay?

What metadata are needed to trace transformations, omissions, uncertainty, and permissions across heterogeneous intelligent units?

**Research approach:** Provenance integrity tests with deliberately corrupted or incomplete chains.

### OQ-047 — How should the system represent irreducible disagreement?

When sources disagree because of different models, objectives, or observations, how should the system retain the disagreement without producing false certainty?

**Research approach:** Typed disagreement records, competing hypotheses, and calibration evaluation.

## 11. Architecture and Interoperability

### OQ-048 — What should the minimum interoperable SSICR contract include?

Which fields are mandatory across strategic subjects, strategies, simulations, maps, worldviews, and authorization records?

**Research approach:** Implement independent producers and consumers against SPEC-001 through SPEC-004; measure schema interoperability.

### OQ-049 — How should versions and lineage be managed?

Can a decision be reconstructed after changes to the strategy, simulator, scoring perspective, or worldview?

**Research approach:** End-to-end replay using immutable experiment identifiers and versioned artifacts.

### OQ-050 — What is the appropriate boundary between central control planes and per-node autonomy?

Which controls must be global, which may be local, and how can conflicts be resolved without erasing subject sovereignty?

**Research approach:** Compare centralized, federated, and hybrid control designs on compliance, performance, and failure containment.

### OQ-051 — What does an auditable strategic Relay trace require?

Can a trace expose enough for review while protecting legitimately sensitive information?

**Research approach:** Tiered disclosure, cryptographic integrity where appropriate, and audit completeness tests.

## 12. Measurement and Validation Questions

### OQ-052 — What benchmark best tests SSICR's distinctive claims?

Can competitive AI R&D organizations serve as a bounded, reproducible testbed without being mistaken for a faithful model of actual companies?

**Research approach:** Begin with synthetic organizations and clearly specified environments, then validate selected mechanisms externally.

### OQ-053 — What baselines are necessary?

Should SSICR be compared against fixed strategies, ordinary multi-agent reinforcement learning, agent-based modeling, single-objective optimizers, and non-structural LLM-agent workflows?

**Research approach:** Pre-register matched-budget baselines and report implementation differences.

### OQ-054 — Which results would disconfirm the central thesis?

Possible disconfirming outcomes include: maps do not generalize beyond simulation; worldview forests increase lock-in; structural Relay adds complexity without benefit; governance contracts fail to prevent unauthorized action; or human users understand fewer tradeoffs despite richer outputs.

**Research approach:** Make these failure criteria first-class benchmark outcomes rather than exceptions.

### OQ-055 — How should cost and complexity be accounted for?

Do the gains in strategic quality justify compute, engineering, interpretability, and governance overhead?

**Research approach:** Report performance per unit compute, implementation cost, review time, and reliability—not only peak scores.

### OQ-056 — How can external validity be established responsibly?

What observations, field studies, or expert assessments are required before claiming utility in real organizations or institutions?

**Research approach:** Staged validation, informed participation, and explicit restrictions on high-stakes deployment.

## 13. Cross-Cutting Research Tensions

| Tension | Why it matters | Research response |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration vs exploitation | Search may converge before rare strategies are found | Diversity-preserving search and held-out evaluation |
| Openness vs confidentiality | Relay benefits can conflict with data and strategic boundaries | Configurable permissions and controlled disclosure |
| Optimization vs pluralism | A single score may erase legitimate goals | Perspective-indexed maps and partial orders |
| Simulation scale vs validity | More rollouts do not correct wrong assumptions | Multi-model testing and external evidence |
| Worldview stability vs revision | Rigidity blocks learning; volatility destroys continuity | Versioned, evidence-triggered revision |
| Autonomy vs sovereignty | Faster actions can outpace meaningful oversight | Risk-tiered, revocable authorization |
| Compression vs fidelity | Summaries may omit decisive counterexamples | Traceable summaries and drill-down evidence |
| Structural growth vs reliability | New components create new failure modes | Contract conformance and rollback |

## 15. Minimal Experiments to Start With

### Experiment A — Strategy ranking reversal

Construct synthetic environments in which Open, Closed, and Hybrid Relay have known context-dependent payoffs. Test whether a learned SAM detects ranking reversals and correctly reports uncertainty near boundaries.

**Pass condition:** Improvement over a fixed global-ranking baseline on held-out environments, with calibrated uncertainty.  
**Disconfirming result:** Maps appear accurate only on training scenarios.

### Experiment B — Adaptive compute versus uniform search

Use the same simulation budget to compare uniform allocation and adaptive uncertainty-based allocation. Measure best validated strategy, coverage of failure regions, and sensitivity to rare events.

**Pass condition:** Better validated coverage or decision value at comparable cost.  
**Disconfirming result:** Adaptive allocation consistently misses important low-frequency failures.

### Experiment C — Counter-evidence retention

Inject plausible contradictory observations into an established advantage map and worldview. Test whether the system preserves provenance, revises applicable claims, and avoids unauthorized value changes.

**Pass condition:** Correct evidence updates and invariant-preserving revisions.  
**Disconfirming result:** Evidence is ignored or normative goals change without authorization.

### Experiment D — Strategic sovereignty under delegation

Run simulated strategic decisions with nested Relay delegates, changing permissions, and revocation events. Measure prohibited actions, trace completeness, and revocation latency.

**Pass condition:** No unauthorized new actions after effective revocation within specified system guarantees.  
**Disconfirming result:** Downstream delegates retain usable authority contrary to the contract.

### Experiment E — Human comprehension under strategy overload

Compare unstructured strategy reports, score-only rankings, and evidence-linked SAM/worldview summaries. Evaluate participants' understanding of tradeoffs, failure boundaries, and authorization implications.

**Pass condition:** Improved calibrated comprehension without hiding material dissent or uncertainty.  
**Disconfirming result:** The structured interface increases unjustified confidence or automation bias.

## 16. Research Artifacts and Reproducibility Requirements

For every experimental claim, record at minimum:

1. Research question and pre-registered hypothesis.
2. Strategy definitions, subject contracts, and authority scopes.
3. Simulator version, scenario generator, random seeds, and compute budget.
4. Scoring perspectives, comparators, and aggregation choices.
5. Training, validation, and held-out evaluation partitions.
6. Evidence provenance, counter-evidence, and unresolved OTHERS.
7. Uncertainty, failure boundaries, and known model limitations.
8. Whether results are synthetic, observational, experimental, or speculative.
9. Reproduction instructions and artifact identifiers.
10. Authorization and safety review for any real-world intervention.

A positive result without these records should not be promoted into a general strategic principle.

## 17. What SSICR Does Not Yet Establish

At version 1.0.0, SSICR is a proposed structural research framework. It does **not** establish that:

- A large number of simulations yields accurate real-world strategic forecasts.
- A universally optimal strategy or worldview exists.
- An evolved worldview is morally superior because it scores well.
- Strategic RSI necessarily produces monotonic or unbounded improvements.
- A simulated social institution is representative of a real institution.
- Human sovereignty is guaranteed by a nominal approval interface.
- The proposed contracts have been independently implemented or empirically validated.

These limitations are research obligations, not defects to be concealed.

## 18. Questions for External Collaborators

Researchers in multi-agent learning, game theory, organizational science, economics, institutional modeling, HCI, security, and AI governance are invited to challenge the framework with concrete questions:

- Which SSICR constructs are already expressible in established methods, and where is new representation actually needed?
- Which assumptions are too strong for realistic strategic environments?
- Which benchmarks can distinguish structural learning from ordinary parameter or policy optimization?
- Which counterexamples invalidate proposed transfer principles?
- Which governance mechanisms provide measurable, not ceremonial, human control?
- What evidence would justify expanding from synthetic organizational settings to real-world claims?

Independent negative results and alternative models are valuable contributions.

## 20. Conclusion

The decisive open question for SSICR is not whether AI can generate more strategies or execute more simulations. It is whether large-scale strategic exploration can yield **conditional, reproducible, evidence-linked, contestable strategic understanding** that improves human judgment without silently transferring authority over goals and futures.

SSICR therefore treats open questions, counter-evidence, negative results, and governance boundaries as permanent parts of the intelligence architecture. The research program should advance by testing—and when necessary rejecting—its own structural hypotheses.
