## Overview

SSICR proposes a structural research framework for strategic intelligence in settings where intelligent subjects cooperate, compete, adapt to one another, and revise their strategies and institutional relationships. It extends cooperative intelligence-unit Relay into **competitive and mixed-motive UIRTU-Relay**, while keeping the authority to set goals and authorize consequential actions with the appropriate human or organizational subjects.

The central research question is:

> When the generation, simulation, competition, and evolution of AI strategies outpace direct human comprehension, how can we use large-scale AI computation to expand strategic understanding without surrendering human strategic sovereignty?

Rather than claiming a universally optimal strategy, SSICR investigates **context-dependent comparative advantage**: which strategy works better, for which subject, against which alternatives and opponents, under which environmental assumptions, according to whose scoring perspective, and with what evidence and uncertainty.

## Key Contributions

### 1. Strategic subjects and competitive Relay

Models distinct strategic subjects with explicit identity, objectives, resources, permissions, evaluation perspectives, and Relay boundaries. Supports cooperative, competitive, adversarial, and hybrid relationships without presuming a single shared objective.

### 2. Massive multi-strategy simulation

Proposes an experimental architecture for strategy generation, cross-play, opponent adaptation, multi-fidelity simulation, adaptive compute allocation, and independent evaluation. Simulated outcomes remain conditional on the model and are **not** presented as real-world empirical findings.

### 3. Strategic Advantage Maps (SAM)

Defines structured, evidence-linked maps of comparative advantage, including context, comparator, opponent, horizon, scoring perspective, failure boundary, uncertainty, and counter-evidence. A map records **where and why** a strategy may be advantageous, not merely who won a simulated contest.

### 4. Strategic Worldview Forests

Explores how validated and qualified advantage findings may inform transferable strategic principles and plural, revisable worldview trees. Empirical belief revision is separated from normative changes to goals and values, which require appropriate authorization.

### 5. Open-ended strategic structural RSI

Examines coupled evolution across intelligence units, Relay topology, strategies, inter-subject relationships, and institutions. Structural novelty is treated as a candidate improvement until tested against baselines and external evidence.

### 6. Human strategic sovereignty

Proposes bounded delegation, attributable authority, reviewable strategic recommendations, revocation, audit trails, and safeguards against unauthorized goal changes. The intended role of AI is to expand the futures humans can understand and choose—not silently decide those futures for them.

## Research Status and Limitations

**v1.0.0 is a conceptual research and specification release.** It defines hypotheses, architectural proposals, data contracts, case designs, and validation plans. It does not establish that SSICR mechanisms outperform baselines in deployed systems, nor that social simulations can reliably predict real institutions. Formal claims of performance, generalization, or governance effectiveness require independent experiments and appropriate external validation.

The proposed approach expressly incorporates counter-evidence, uncertainty, unknown residual factors (`OTHERS`), anti-Goodhart safeguards, and separate authorization for consequential real-world actions.
