## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-22

### Added — Research Framework

Initial research release of **SSICR: Strategic Structural Intelligence and Competitive UIRTU-Relay**, subtitled *From Multi-Strategy Simulation to Comparative Advantage Maps, Strategic Worldviews, and Human Strategic Sovereignty*.

The framework extends cooperative intelligence relay into settings with competing, cooperating, and mixed-motive strategic subjects. It proposes a research pipeline from strategic subjects and relay contracts through large-scale multi-strategy simulation, conditional comparative advantage, worldview formation, and open-ended structural improvement, subject to human-governed authority boundaries.

### Clarified — Research and Governance Boundaries

- **Simulation performance is not real-world proof.** Cross-model and external validation remain necessary.
- **Comparative advantage is conditional.** Rankings must identify the subject, comparator, context, scoring perspective, and evidence.
- **Worldview updates are not value-authority transfers.** Empirical belief revision and normative goal changes are distinct operations.
- **Delegated computation does not confer sovereign authority.** Consequential actions remain subject to explicit, reviewable, and revocable authorization.
- **Counter-evidence and OTHERS remain first-class objects.** Uncertainty and unresolved evidence must not be silently discarded.
- **Structural growth is not automatically improvement.** Claims of improved intelligence require independent evaluation.

## Versioning and Citation Policy

- **Major** (`X.0.0`): Substantial revision to the core research architecture, assumptions, or normative contracts.
- **Minor** (`1.X.0`): New research modules, schemas, cases, or validation protocols compatible with the current conceptual architecture.
- **Patch** (`1.0.X`): Editorial corrections, broken-link repairs, figure fixes, and non-substantive clarifications.

Each archival release should have an immutable Git tag and a version-specific citation. The repository DOI, if assigned, may point to the evolving project, while version-specific DOIs identify fixed releases. Never substitute a pending DOI with a fabricated identifier.
