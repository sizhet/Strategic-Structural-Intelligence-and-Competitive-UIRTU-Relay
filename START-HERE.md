## 1. Welcome

This guide helps researchers, engineers, and decision-makers navigate **Strategic Structural Intelligence and Competitive UIRTU-Relay (SSICR)**. The repository develops a structural approach to competitive and mixed-motive interactions among strategic subjects that may possess different capabilities, objectives, resources, worldviews, and decision rights.

SSICR extends cooperative intelligence relay into **strategic interaction**: participants may cooperate, compete, adapt to opponents, negotiate interfaces, alter institutions, and revise their strategic understanding. It proposes a disciplined way to use large-scale AI computation to explore this space without treating simulated optimization as an authorization to act.

**The central question:**

> When AI can generate, simulate, compare, and evolve strategies faster than humans can directly comprehend them, how can we use that computational power while preserving the strategic sovereignty of the subjects whose futures are at stake?

SSICR's proposed answer combines **multi-strategy simulation**, **contextual strategic advantage maps**, **strategic worldview forests**, **open-ended structural learning**, and **explicit human authority boundaries**.

This is a **research proposal**, not a claim that the architecture has already been implemented or empirically validated.

## 2. The Project in One Minute

A conventional optimizer may ask: *Which strategy scores highest?*

SSICR instead asks:

1. **Whose strategy?** Identify the subject, its goals, authority, and boundaries.
2. **Against whom and under what conditions?** Model competitors, partners, institutions, and changing environments.
3. **According to which scoring perspective?** Make evaluation criteria and their owners explicit.
4. **What does the evidence actually support?** Preserve uncertainty, counter-evidence, failure boundaries, and unknown factors.
5. **What can be learned and transferred?** Convert repeated findings into conditional advantage maps and revisable strategic principles.
6. **Who may decide and act?** Keep simulation, recommendation, authorization, and execution distinct.

The goal is **better structured strategic understanding**, not an unconditional or universal winning strategy.

## 3. Conceptual Architecture

```text
Strategic Subjects + Goals + Authority Boundaries
                     |
                     v
     Cooperative / Competitive / Hybrid UIRTU-Relay
                     |
                     v
   Strategy Generation + Scenarios + Opponent Models
                     |
                     v
      Massive Multi-Strategy Simulation & Cross-Play
                     |
                     v
  Multi-Perspective Scoring + Counter-Evidence + OTHERS
                     |
                     v
        Strategic Advantage Maps (SAM)
                     |
                     v
      Conditional Principles + Worldview Forests
                     |
                     v
       Open-Ended Strategic Structural RSI
                     |
                     +----> New strategies, relay forms,
                     |      evidence, models, institutions
                     |
                     v
     Human Review, Authorization, and Governance
```

Feedback is bidirectional. New evidence can invalidate prior advantage claims; revised worldviews can change which strategies are explored; institutional changes can reshape the competitive environment. **Authority to revise empirical beliefs does not imply authority to change a subject's normative goals.**

## 5. Seven Essential Terms

| Term | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strategic subject** | An identifiable agent, organization, or other decision-bearing entity with objectives, boundaries, and attributable authority. |
| **Competitive UIRTU-Relay** | Intelligence-unit interaction and transfer under cooperative, adversarial, or mixed-motive strategic relationships. |
| **Scoring perspective** | An explicitly identified set of evaluation criteria, priorities, constraints, and owners. |
| **Strategic Advantage Map (SAM)** | A structured record of *where, for whom, against what, and with what evidence* a strategy has comparative advantage. |
| **Strategic Worldview Forest** | A plural collection of revisable strategic principles, valuations, and subject-specific interpretations. |
| **OTHERS** | An explicit space for unknown, unmodeled, weakly observed, or not-yet-classified factors. |
| **Human Strategic Sovereignty** | Retention of accountable human authority over consequential objectives, delegation, authorization, and revocation. |

## 8. What Counts as Evidence?

SSICR deliberately separates four categories:

- **Proposed mechanism:** an architectural idea or formal contract to be evaluated.
- **Illustrative scenario:** a synthetic example explaining how the mechanism might work.
- **Simulation result:** a result from an actually executed, documented computational experiment.
- **Externally validated finding:** a claim supported by independent data, replication, or appropriate out-of-sample evaluation.

The papers, schemas, and cases in this release principally develop the first two categories. No example scores, synthetic organizations, or scenario narratives should be presented as established empirical findings.

A credible validation program should include baseline comparisons, opponent adaptation, held-out scenarios, sensitivity analyses, uncertainty calibration, evidence provenance, adversarial counter-evidence, and governance audits. See [TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md](TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md) and [VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md](VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md) when available.

## 9. Five Questions to Keep in View

1. Does a proposed advantage survive a change in opponents, environment, or scoring perspective?
2. Can the system distinguish a robust conditional principle from an overfit simulation artifact?
3. Can unknown factors and counter-evidence trigger revision rather than being suppressed?
4. Can strategic worldviews evolve without collapsing legitimate differences in goals and values?
5. Can humans inspect, reject, revoke, or redirect consequential recommendations before action?

These questions should guide both technical experiments and normative evaluation.

## 10. Suggested First Research Experiment

A manageable starting point is a **synthetic competitive AI R&D organization benchmark**:

1. Define three or four organizations with different resources, goals, and authority boundaries.
2. Compare open, closed, and hybrid relay policies under multiple environmental conditions.
3. Include fixed and adaptive opponents, with repeated random seeds.
4. Score results under multiple explicitly owned perspectives.
5. Record conditional advantages, failure regions, counter-evidence, and uncertainty in SAM objects.
6. Test whether a derived principle generalizes to held-out scenarios.
7. Require human review for any proposed change in organizational objectives or external action.

**Success is not merely a high score.** It is a reproducible, auditable improvement in strategic understanding, with clear limits and preserved decision authority.

## 13. Closing Perspective

The strategic value of AI is not exhausted by generating more candidate plans or selecting higher-scoring actions. A deeper opportunity is to construct an **open, inspectable, evidence-sensitive intelligence system** that helps subjects understand strategic alternatives, discover conditional comparative advantages, revise mistaken principles, and adapt to changing competitive environments.

**The intended destination is greater human strategic capacity—not the silent transfer of human strategic sovereignty to an optimizer.**
