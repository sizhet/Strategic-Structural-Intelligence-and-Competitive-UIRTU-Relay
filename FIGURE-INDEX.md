## 3. Figure-by-Figure Research Interpretation

### Figure 001 — SSICR Grand Architecture

**File:** `figures/Fig-001-SSICR-Grand-Architecture.png`

**Intended visual:** Strategic subjects and their UIRTU units connect through cooperative, competitive, adversarial and mixed-motive Relay relationships. A simulation-and-scoring loop yields evidence-conditioned Strategic Advantage Maps, candidate strategic principles, Worldview Forests, and proposals for structural RSI. Human strategic sovereignty governs authorization and real-world action. Counter-evidence and OTHERS provide feedback across the stack.

**Key distinction:** Simulation outputs and proposed actions are not equivalent to authorized actions.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-010-Unified-Architecture-and-Research-Horizon.md`, immediately after its unified architecture introduction. **Secondary placements:** `README.md`, `START-HERE.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 001. Unified SSICR architecture: strategic subjects, competitive Relay, simulation, comparative-advantage knowledge, worldview evolution, and human-governed structural improvement.*

### Figure 002 — Cooperative to Competitive Relay

**File:** `figures/Fig-002-Cooperative-to-Competitive-Relay.png`

**Intended visual:** A transition from cooperative knowledge sharing to a continuum of reciprocal, competitive, adversarial and hybrid Relay configurations. Show asymmetric incentives, selective disclosure, trust boundaries, opponent adaptation and possible collaboration under competition.

**Key distinction:** Competition changes the incentives and authority contracts of Relay; it does not eliminate the possibility of cooperation.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-001-From-Cooperative-to-Strategic-Relay.md`, after the discussion introducing competitive Relay. **Secondary placements:** `docs/SSICR-002-Strategic-Subjects-and-Relay-Boundaries.md`, `cases/CASE-002-Open-Closed-and-Hybrid-Relay-Strategies.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 002. Extending cooperative Relay to strategic interactions with heterogeneous incentives, selective interfaces, and adaptive opponents.*

### Figure 003 — Strategic Subjects and Control Planes

**File:** `figures/Fig-003-Strategic-Subjects-and-Control-Planes.png`

**Intended visual:** Multiple subjects, each with identity, goals, capabilities, internal scoring, boundaries, Relay permissions and an authority-bearing control plane. Distinguish local decision control from shared protocols, and indicate authorization, audit and revocation pathways.

**Key distinction:** A strategic subject is not interchangeable with an autonomous agent or with a scoring component; decision authority has an explicit owner.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-003-Strategic-Control-Planes-and-Scoring.md`, following its control-plane architecture. **Secondary placements:** `docs/SSICR-002-Strategic-Subjects-and-Relay-Boundaries.md`, `specs/SPEC-001-Strategic-Subject-and-Relay-Contract.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 003. Subject-specific control and scoring planes with explicit Relay boundaries, authorization, and auditability.*

### Figure 004 — Massive Multi-Strategy Simulation

**File:** `figures/Fig-004-Massive-Multi-Strategy-Simulation.png`

**Intended visual:** Strategy and scenario generation; candidate strategy portfolios; multi-agent self-play/cross-play; opponent adaptation; multi-fidelity simulation; multi-perspective scoring; uncertainty analysis; counter-evidence; external validation; and feedback to strategy generation. Indicate that adaptive compute allocation focuses on uncertain or decision-relevant regions.

**Key distinction:** Large simulation volume alone does not establish external validity.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-004-Massive-Multi-Strategy-Simulation.md`, after its simulation pipeline. **Secondary placements:** `specs/SPEC-002-Strategy-and-Simulation-Schema.md`, `cases/CASE-001-Competitive-AI-RD-Organizations.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 004. Multi-strategy simulation and evaluation pipeline, including opponent adaptation, counter-evidence, and validation gates.*

### Figure 005 — Strategic Advantage Landscape

**File:** `figures/Fig-005-Strategic-Advantage-Landscape.png`

**Intended visual:** A conditional advantage landscape partitioned by context, opponent, time horizon, resources and scoring perspective. Distinguish regions where strategies outperform comparators, uncertain transition regions, documented failure boundaries, and unobserved OTHERS space. Use a legend to separate evidence-backed regions from hypothetical illustration.

**Key distinction:** No strategy is globally superior merely because it wins under one simulation distribution or scoring perspective.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-005-Strategic-Comparative-Advantage-Maps.md`, after the definition of advantage regions and failure boundaries. **Secondary placements:** `specs/SPEC-003-Strategic-Advantage-Map-Schema.md`, `cases/CASE-002-Open-Closed-and-Hybrid-Relay-Strategies.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 005. Context-conditioned strategic comparative advantage, uncertainty, and failure boundaries; schematic rather than empirical.*

### Figure 006 — Strategic Worldview Forest

**File:** `figures/Fig-006-Strategic-Worldview-Forest.png`

**Intended visual:** Multiple branches of strategic principles derived from evidence-conditioned advantage maps, with different subject perspectives, normative priorities, and epistemic assumptions. Show provenance, counter-evidence, revision, branching and coexistence rather than compulsory convergence.

**Key distinction:** Revising empirical beliefs does not automatically authorize changing a subject's values or goals.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-006-Strategic-Worldview-Forests.md`, following its map-to-principle-to-worldview explanation. **Secondary placements:** `specs/SPEC-004-Worldview-and-Counter-Evidence-Schema.md`, `cases/CASE-003-Strategic-Worldview-Evolution.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 006. A pluralistic Strategic Worldview Forest: evidence-backed principles, competing perspectives, counter-evidence, and governed revision.*

### Figure 007 — Social Structural Co-Evolution

**File:** `figures/Fig-007-Social-Structural-Co-Evolution.png`

**Intended visual:** Three coupled evolutionary levels: internal capabilities and policies of strategic subjects; intersubject Relay networks, alliances and competition; and institutions, norms, standards and governance. Depict feedback in both directions and changes over time.

**Key distinction:** Institutions are not merely a fixed simulation environment; they can constrain, emerge from, and be changed by subject interaction.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-007-Social-Structural-Simulation-and-Co-Evolution.md`, after its three-level co-evolution model. **Secondary placements:** `docs/SSICR-009-Open-Ended-Strategic-RSI.md`, `cases/CASE-001-Competitive-AI-RD-Organizations.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 007. Coupled evolution of subject intelligence, strategic Relay relationships, and institutional structures.*

### Figure 008 — Human Strategic Sovereignty

**File:** `figures/Fig-008-Human-Strategic-Sovereignty.png`

**Intended visual:** AI-generated strategy streams are distilled into intelligible options, supporting evidence, uncertainties, risks, counter-evidence and tradeoffs. A human-governed control plane sets goals, delegates bounded tasks, authorizes consequential action, and retains monitoring, revocation and appeal mechanisms.

**Key distinction:** Advice, simulation, recommendation, authorization and execution are separate stages with different permissions.

**Primary placement:** `docs/SSICR-008-Human-Strategic-Sovereignty.md`, after the governance/authority architecture. **Secondary placements:** `docs/SSICR-010-Unified-Architecture-and-Research-Horizon.md`, `specs/SPEC-004-Worldview-and-Counter-Evidence-Schema.md`.

**Suggested caption:** *Figure 008. AI expands the human strategic choice space while authorization, value commitments, and consequential action remain subject to accountable governance.*
